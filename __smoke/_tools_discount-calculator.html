How the stacked discounts apply

Each discount comes off the price that is left, not off the original.

  1. Step 1 — 20% off−200 → 800
  2. Step 2 — 10% off−80 → 720

20% then 10% is 28% off, not 30% — the second discount is calculated on the already-reduced price. The retailer is not hiding anything; the arithmetic is just not additive.

How to use Discount Calculator

  1. 1Enter the price and the first discount percentage. Add a second percentage to stack discounts.
  2. 2Compare the stacked result against what the two percentages would give if they simply added up — the tool shows both.
  3. 3Read the effective single percentage, which is what the stacked discounts actually come to.
  4. 4Add pack sizes and prices to the comparison table to see which is cheapest per unit.

Discount Calculator — frequently asked questions

Why is 20% then 10% not 30% off?

Because the second discount is calculated on the price that is left, not on the original. 20% then 10% is 28% off. The tool shows the flat total beside it so the gap is visible.

Is the retailer misleading me with stacked discounts?

Not usually — the arithmetic simply is not additive. The useful question is what the final price is, and that is the number the tool leads with.

What if I know the sale price but not the original?

Enter the discounted price and the discount percentage, and the tool works the original back out.

How does the pack comparison handle different units?

It divides price by size and labels the result per unit, whatever that unit is. A pack with a missing or zero size is skipped rather than given an invented price.
